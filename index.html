Stop picking models

What IBM Bob is, and how it decides which AI model handles each task, so your developers don't have to.

SubbuNovember 2026 · 4 min read

A new model tops the leaderboard every few weeks.

Teams argue about it in chat. Someone runs a quick comparison. A favourite gets pinned, and from then on the same model renames a variable and redesigns a payment service.

That's two problems at once. You overpay for the small jobs, and you may be underpowered for the big ones.

The question isn't which model is best.

It's which model is best for this task, at this cost, right now.

01 · The partner

First, what is IBM Bob?

IBM Bob is an AI development partner. That's a deliberate step up from a coding assistant.

An assistant sits in your editor and finishes the line you're typing. Bob works across the whole software lifecycle. It explores a codebase, plans changes, writes and tests code, and documents what it did, using focused agents that each have their own context, tools and skills.

You stay in charge. Bob asks for approval before it changes your code, unless you've told it a type of task can go ahead on its own.

03 · How it works

How the router decides

Think of triage in a hospital. One assessment at the door decides who sees you. After that, you stay with the same doctor, because they already know your case.

IBM Bob's router works the same way. It looks at the first request of a task and matches the work to a model in one of three tiers. The tier decides how many Bobcoins the task costs.

Bobcoins are Bob's standard currency. They absorb the swings in token prices and model capabilities, so you don't have to track them.

04 · The vetting

How a model earns its place

A model doesn't get into IBM Bob because it tops a leaderboard. Quality, performance and cost are all gates, and each new model passes through five of them.

05 · Why it matters

If you run an engineering team

  • Focus. Developers stop debating models and get on with the work.
  • Budget. You plan in Bobcoins, not in token prices that keep moving.
  • Evidence. Models are judged on real engineering work, not on vendor claims.
  • Review. Every model passes a human check before it reaches your team.
THE HONEST TRADE-OFF All that vetting takes time. The newest model won't appear in Bob the day it launches, and the Bob team says so openly. If your developers want this week's leaderboard winner on day one, they'll be frustrated. For enterprise software, I think that's the right call.

Models will keep changing. That's exactly why choosing them shouldn't be your developers' job.

Stop managing models. Start managing outcomes.
